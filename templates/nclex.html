NCLEX guide

What is the NCLEX?

The National Council Licensure Examination is the official licensing exam for nurses who want to practise in countries such as the United States and Canada. It exists to check that a nurse is competent, safe and ready for real clinical work.

{# ── Two exams ────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

Which one

There are two NCLEX exams.

Which one you take depends on the kind of nurse you trained to be.

Most likely yours

NCLEX-RN

For Registered Nurses. This is the exam most Ghanaian nurses will take, and the one this guide is mainly about.

Less common here

NCLEX-PN

For Practical and Vocational Nurses. Not often taken by Ghana-trained nurses.

{# ── Adaptive testing ─────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

The format

The exam adapts to you.

The NCLEX uses Computer Adaptive Testing. There is no fixed paper. Each question is picked based on how you answered the one before.

Answer correctly and the next question gets harder.

Answer incorrectly and the next one gets easier.

The aim is to find your competency level, which is why the exam can end anywhere between 75 and 145 questions.

{# ── What is tested ───────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

The content

What you'll be tested on.

Every question maps to one of four areas of the Client Needs framework.

01

Safe and Effective Care Environment

Management of care, safety and infection control.

02

Health Promotion and Maintenance

Growth and development, prevention and screening.

03

Psychosocial Integrity

Mental health, coping and adaptation.

04

Physiological Integrity

Basic care, pharmacology, reduction of risk and complex care.

{# ── Question types ───────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

The questions

Five kinds of question.

It isn't all multiple choice. Practise every format before exam day.

MCQ

Multiple choice

Pick the one best answer.

SATA

Select all that apply

Choose every correct option, however many there are.

1.5

Fill in the blank

Type a calculated answer, such as a dosage or a drip rate.

A→B

Drag and drop

Put steps or priorities into the right order.

Case

Case-based scenarios

Read a patient case, then answer questions about it.

{# ── Route to the exam ────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

Getting to the exam

From graduate to test day.

You need to have graduated from a recognised nursing school, and you apply through a nursing board, for example a U.S. state board. Once the board approves you, you receive your Authorization to Test. You'll also need to show identification and, where it's required, proof of English.

  1. Apply to a nursing board

    For example Texas or New York.

  2. Register with Pearson VUE

    They run the exam and the test centres.

  3. Receive your ATT

    Your Authorization to Test.

  4. Schedule the exam

    Pick a date and a test centre.

  5. Take the exam

    Up to five hours, on a computer.

Practical details

Cost, place and results.

Exam fee

$200 USD

That's the base fee. Budget for application fees, credential evaluation and travel to a testing centre on top.

Where

Pearson VUE

The NCLEX is written at Pearson VUE test centres around the world, including in Africa and nearby countries.

Results

48 hours

Quick results are an optional paid service. Your official result is sent by the nursing board.

{# ── Habits ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

How to pass

Five habits that get nurses through.

01

Practise every day

Work through NCLEX-style questions daily. Little and often beats one long session at the weekend.

02

Think, don't memorise

Most questions ask what to do first or next. You have to reason it out from what you know.

03

Learn to prioritise

Airway, breathing, circulation and Maslow's hierarchy settle many "which patient first" questions.

04

Read every rationale

Right or wrong, the explanation is where the learning is. Ask why each of the other options is wrong.

05

Sit timed mock exams

Build the stamina for a long computer-based exam and get used to the pace.
